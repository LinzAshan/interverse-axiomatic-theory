# Interverse Axiomatic Theory (IAT)

### Evidence over hype.

Interverse Axiomatic Theory (IAT) is an evidence-first analytical framework for evaluating fictional characters, abilities, power systems, cosmologies, and cross-universe matchups.

IAT is designed for rigorous verse debates where conclusions should be determined by **demonstrated evidence, underlying mechanisms, and logical compatibility** rather than popularity, reputation, narrative assumptions, or hype.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of IAT is to determine how strongly the available canon supports a particular conclusion.

IAT does not begin with:

> "Who is stronger?"

It begins with:

> **"What does the available evidence actually establish?"**

The framework separates what is demonstrated from what is inferred, assumed, or merely possible.

---

## Core Principles

### 1. Analysis Before Conclusion

A verdict is the result of the analysis, not the starting point.

IAT does not select a winner first and then construct arguments around that conclusion.

---

### 2. Profile Before Comparison (PBC)

Each character, ability, and power system is analyzed within its own verse before cross-verse comparison begins.

This establishes:

- What the character can demonstrably do
- How their abilities function
- Their limitations and conditions
- Their demonstrated scaling
- Their relevant resistances
- Their interaction with their own verse's mechanics

Only after this profile is established should comparison begin.

---

### 3. Mechanism-First Analysis

IAT analyzes **how an ability actually works**, rather than relying on its name, appearance, reputation, or broad classification.

An ability being described as:

- "absolute"
- "infinite"
- "conceptual"
- "reality-warping"
- "omnipotent"

does not automatically establish every possible interpretation of that description.

The actual demonstrated mechanism takes priority.

---

### 4. Verse Compatibility

Different fictional universes operate under different rules.

Before assuming that one character's ability affects another character, IAT examines whether the relevant mechanics are compatible.

The question is not simply:

> "Can this ability bypass the opponent?"

It is:

> **"Does the established mechanism of this ability provide a demonstrated or logically supported pathway for affecting this particular target under the conditions of the comparison?"**

---

### 5. Evidence Over Narrative Assumption

IAT distinguishes between:

**Demonstrated**

What canon directly establishes.

**Supported inference**

What can reasonably be derived from established evidence.

**Possibility**

Something that may be possible but lacks sufficient evidence to establish it.

**Speculation**

An interpretation that depends primarily on assumptions rather than demonstrated mechanics.

Narrative expectation is not treated as mechanical evidence.

---

## Evidence-Weighted Reasoning

Not every piece of evidence has equal value.

IAT therefore weighs evidence according to its reliability, relevance, clarity, and relationship to the specific claim being evaluated.

A conclusion becomes stronger when multiple independent pieces of evidence converge on the same result.

Conversely, uncertainty increases when:

- Evidence is contradictory
- A mechanism is incompletely explained
- A proposed interaction has no demonstrated precedent
- The conclusion depends on several unverified assumptions
- Different interpretations of the canon remain viable

---

## Two-Pass Methodology

IAT uses a two-pass analytical process.

### Pass One — Strict IAT Analysis

The matchup is analyzed using the established IAT framework.

This includes:

1. Character profiling
2. Ability and mechanism analysis
3. Relevant scaling
4. Compatibility analysis
5. Win conditions
6. Limitations and countermeasures
7. Evidence evaluation
8. Preliminary verdict

The objective is to determine what the evidence itself supports.

### Pass Two — Comparative Validation / Stress Test

The preliminary conclusion is then challenged.

Alternative interpretations, counterarguments, interaction problems, and competing win conditions are examined.

The purpose is not to force the conclusion to change.

The purpose is to determine whether the conclusion survives serious scrutiny.

---

## Evidence-Weighted Confidence

IAT does not treat every verdict as equally certain.

A conclusion may be:

- Strongly supported
- Moderately supported
- Weakly supported
- Indeterminate

The strength of a verdict depends on the quality and quantity of evidence supporting the relevant interaction.

A numerical score should therefore represent **analytical confidence**, not an artificial claim of mathematical certainty.

---

## Cross-Verse Analysis

Cross-verse debates require particular care because two universes may define concepts such as:

- Energy
- Souls
- Space
- Time
- Mind
- Life
- Death
- Causality
- Dimensions
- Physical interaction
- Spiritual interaction
- Reality alteration

in fundamentally different ways.

IAT does not automatically assume that identical terminology means identical mechanics.

Each interaction must be examined according to what the respective canon actually establishes.

---

## What IAT Does Not Do

### No Popularity Scaling

A character does not become stronger because they are more popular.

### No Hype Scaling

Statements or fan interpretations are not automatically treated as proof of capability.

### No Narrative Scaling

What a story appears likely to do in the future is not automatically evidence of what a character can currently accomplish.

### No Automatic Cross-Verse Equalization

Verse equalization cannot simply be assumed to grant an ability properties that its canon has not established.

### No Forced Winners

If the evidence cannot reliably establish a winner, IAT allows the conclusion to remain uncertain.

---

## The IAT Standard

A strong IAT argument should be able to answer:

1. **What is being claimed?**
2. **What evidence supports the claim?**
3. **What mechanism produces the relevant effect?**
4. **What limitations or conditions apply?**
5. **Does the opponent possess a relevant resistance or counter?**
6. **Is the interaction compatible across the two verses?**
7. **What assumptions are required?**
8. **How strong is the evidence supporting the final conclusion?**
9. **Can the conclusion survive opposing interpretations?**

If an argument cannot answer these questions, its confidence should be reduced accordingly.

---

## IAT and Verse Debates

IAT is designed to provide a structured way to approach debates involving:

- Power scaling
- Character matchups
- Ability interactions
- Hax analysis
- Cosmology
- Speed and reaction comparisons
- Durability and attack potency
- Resistances
- Win conditions
- Cross-verse mechanics
- Verse compatibility
- Alternative interpretations

The objective is not to make every matchup produce a definitive winner.

The objective is to determine **what the evidence can actually justify**.

---

## Guiding Principle

> **Evidence determines the strength of the conclusion.**

IAT does not exist to confirm a preferred character, verse, or interpretation.

It exists to test the argument.

When new canon changes the available evidence, the conclusion can change.

**The method remains consistent.**

---

## Project Status

Interverse Axiomatic Theory is an ongoing analytical framework under development.

The framework may evolve as its methodology is tested, refined, and stress-tested across increasingly complex fictional power systems.

**Current principle:**

> **Analyze the evidence. Test the mechanism. Challenge the conclusion.**
